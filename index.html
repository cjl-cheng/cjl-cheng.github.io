About

Understanding model behavior to make it more dependable.

I am an M.Eng. student in Safety Science and Engineering at Beihang University's School of Reliability and Systems Engineering. I previously completed a B.Eng. in Safety Engineering and a second bachelor's degree in Mathematics and Applied Mathematics at Beihang University.

My research asks how internal model mechanisms and behavioral signals can reveal failures that are difficult to detect from final text alone. I work at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability, hallucination detection, trustworthy NLP, reliable reasoning, and formal methods, with the longer-term goal of connecting diagnosis to verification and intervention.

Research interests

Four connected directions

01

Mechanistic interpretability

Studying how internal components and computational mechanisms shape language-model behavior, generalization, and failure.

02

Interpretable failure detection

Using internal and behavioral signals to identify model failures while keeping the resulting evidence understandable and auditable.

03

Reliable reasoning

Investigating why reasoning processes fail and how mechanistic evidence can support more dependable inference and evaluation.

04

Formal methods for AI

Exploring how formal specifications and verification ideas can make language-model reliability claims more precise and inspectable.

Research themes

Questions guiding my work

Mechanistic understanding

How do internal mechanisms shape reliable behavior?

I examine how interpretable internal structure relates to in-context behavior, generalization, and failure.

My goal is to move from descriptive observations toward causal analysis: identifying mechanisms, testing them through controlled interventions, and understanding when the resulting explanations transfer beyond a single setting.

Interpretable reliability analysis

What evidence can make model failures auditable?

I investigate signals that can reveal unreliable behavior without reducing the analysis to an opaque score.

I am particularly interested in compact representations, explicit semantics, and evaluation procedures that expose both the usefulness and limitations of a detector.
