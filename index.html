AI interpretability
and trustworthy AI.

I want to understand how language models use information, why they make mistakes, and how explanations can help people assess and improve their behavior.

I am an M.Eng. student in Safety Science and Engineering at Beihang University’s School of Reliability and Systems Engineering, expecting to graduate in June 2027. My work brings together model analysis, mechanistic interpretability, controlled interventions and interpretable failure detection.

Research experience

My work approaches language-model behavior from two sides: understanding the computations behind it and making evidence of failure easier to inspect.

Context use and failures in language models

Ongoing

Master’s thesis, Beihang University

My thesis asks when language models overlook or misuse evidence in long inputs. In one ongoing study, I change where the same evidence appears and swap internal model states between matched inputs to measure how the model’s support for candidate answers changes.

The broader goal is to understand the mechanisms behind these failures and use that understanding to develop interpretable warnings and test ways to reduce errors.

Positional information in language models

2025 – 2026

Graduate research project, Beihang University

I studied how attention uses position when a model has to answer questions from text that contains distracting information.

  • Proposed and implemented a way to adapt how individual attention heads use position and analyzed the mathematics of the modified computation.
  • Designed controlled interventions in the original model and compared ways of choosing where to intervene to test how much the learned parameters could help.

January 2025 – May 2026; follow-up work in August – September 2026.

Interpretable error detection

2026

Research collaboration following my Verimag placement

When a detector flags a model’s answer, can we check what prompted the warning? I studied this using completed answers to mathematical problems.

  • Designed and implemented rules that look for patterns in how the model’s confidence changes over an answer.
  • Compared detectors on held-out problem groups and used the recorded scores and rule conditions to check what triggered individual warnings.

June 6 – August 1, 2026.
